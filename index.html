Questions

The questions you actually have.

Isn't this just a wrapper for ChatGPT or Claude?
Partly, yes. EchoOS runs on the AI you bring. What it adds is the work around the chat: a video library, timestamped transcripts, clip cutting, voice profiles and a drafts page.
What do I need to run it?
A Windows or Linux computer and your own AI: an OpenRouter key, your ChatGPT plan through Codex sign-in, or your own signed-in Claude Code. Setup walks you through it, and you can skip the AI step and come back later. If you like the terminal, there's a command line for clips too.
What happens to my videos?
The videos you drop in stay where they are. EchoOS doesn't copy them. Videos from a link are saved to EchoOS's own folder. Transcription can run on your computer, or through OpenAI if you pick it, and then the audio goes to OpenAI. Your chats, drafts and the transcripts you work with go to the AI you connect.
What does early access mean?
You talk to me before EchoOS is out in public. On the call, we look at how you make content now, and you see EchoOS work on a real video. Pricing and the public release aren't decided yet, so there's no price here and nothing to download.
Will this get me clients?
No tool can promise that, and I won't. EchoOS helps you get posts out of what you already recorded. What those posts say, and who they reach, is still on you.

You told me you want to get more out of the videos you already make, and right now you're sitting on videos you never used.

That's common when you serve clients and make your own content. You're doing two jobs, and the second one gets whatever time is left.

The bad news

The slow part starts after you hit stop. Find the minute, transcribe it, cut it vertical, write the post. Each step waits for a free afternoon, and in a client week the video stays in the folder.

The good news

EchoOS does that middle part. Paste a link or drop in a file, and you get a timestamped transcript. Ask for clips and get up to five suggestions. Pick one and it's cut to an MP4, vertical included. Ask for a post and it drafts one in your voice.

The bad news

AI writing from a blank prompt has nothing of yours to go on, so it reaches for the most common way to say it. Editing that back into your voice can take as long as writing it yourself.

The good news

EchoOS starts from you. You build a voice profile in setup, and drafts start from the transcript of what you said on camera. It stays a draft until you say it's done. You're the last edit.

The bad news

I build around one rule: each piece of content has one job, to start a conversation with the right person. A post written for everyone gets a like and a scroll. And when one post takes hours, there's no time left to decide its job.

The good news

EchoOS can't pick the job for you, and no tool can promise you clients. It takes the slow part off your plate, so your time goes to what the post says and who it's for.

The bad news

A routine with a handful of manual steps breaks the first busy week. Every step that needs you is a place it stops, and starting again feels like starting from zero.

The good news

EchoOS cuts the steps down. Add a folder and new videos show up on their own, transcribed if you want. Search your transcripts by what was said. Ask for clips or a post in one chat. The post comes back as a draft you can revise.
