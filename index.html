The Engine

From one video to posts you approve.

Five steps. You stay the source and the last edit.

1. Set Up Once

A guided setup connects your AI (skip it and come back later) and interviews you for your voice. If you want, add the folders your videos live in. Then a tour walks you through the real app.

2. Bring a Video

Paste a link or drop in a file. Or point EchoOS at a folder, and new videos show up on their own.

3. Get the Transcript

A timestamped transcript lands on a board. Later, find it again by what was said.

4. Ask for Clips and Posts

Ask for clips and get up to five suggestions. Pick one and it's cut to an MP4, vertical if you want. Ask for a post and it drafts one in your voice.

5. Your Review

Open the draft, revise it, approve it, copy it. Nothing gets posted unless you post it.

The Questions You Actually Have

The Questions You Actually Have

Honest answers. No pitch-spinning.

Is this just a wrapper for Claude or ChatGPT?
Partly, yes. EchoOS runs on the AI you bring: an OpenRouter key, your ChatGPT plan through Codex sign-in, or your own signed-in Claude Code. What it adds is the work around the chat. Your video library, timestamped transcripts, clip cutting, voice profiles and a drafts page. Your AI writes. EchoOS gives it your words to start from and cuts the clip you pick.
Is it a video editor?
It cuts clips, including 9:16 vertical. For anything past a clean cut, keep the editor you use now.
What if I don't like the drafts?
Revise them. Every post is a draft you open, revise, approve and copy. You can keep more than one voice profile and edit any of them. Nothing gets posted unless you post it.
How long until it sounds like me?
Your voice profile comes from an interview during setup, and you can edit it any time. How close the first draft lands depends on you and the AI you connect, so I won't put a number on it. You're the last edit, every time.
What does early access mean?
You talk to me before EchoOS is out in public. Pricing and the public release aren't decided yet, so this page has no price and no download. I'd rather tell you that than make up a date.
What happens on the call?
We look at how you make content now, and you see EchoOS work on a real video. Bring one if you have it.
Do I need to be technical?
No. You need a Windows or Linux computer and your own AI account. A guided setup connects your AI (you can skip that and come back) and interviews you for your voice. If you want, you add your video folders there too. Then a tour shows you the real app. If you like the terminal, there's a command line too.
What happens to my data?
The videos you drop in stay where they are. EchoOS uses your files in place and doesn't copy them into the app. Videos from a link are saved to EchoOS's own folder. Transcription can run on your own computer. If you choose OpenAI for transcription, the audio goes to OpenAI. Your chats and drafts go to the AI you connect.
Does it post or run on its own?
No. It doesn't post for you or message anyone. It works when you ask, with one exception you switch on yourself: a watched folder can transcribe new videos as they land.
Will this get me clients?
No tool can promise that, and I won't. EchoOS helps you get posts out of what you already recorded. What those posts say, and who they reach, is still on you.

You're not behind

You told me you want to get more out of the videos you already make, and right now you're sitting on videos you never turned into anything.

That's a frustrating spot, and it's a common one. When you serve clients and make your own content, you're doing two jobs, and the second one gets whatever time is left.

The bad news

The slow part starts after you hit stop. Finding the good minute, getting it transcribed, cutting it to vertical, writing the post. Each of those is its own manual job, so the whole thing waits for a free afternoon. In a client week, that afternoon doesn't come, and the video sits in a folder.

The good news

EchoOS does that middle step. Paste a YouTube, TikTok or Instagram link, or drop in a file, and you get a timestamped transcript on a board. Ask for clips and you get up to five suggestions. Pick one and it's cut to a playable MP4, vertical 9:16 included. Ask for a post and it drafts one in your voice. Point it at a folder, and new videos show up on their own, transcribed if you want.

The bad news

When AI writes from a blank prompt, it has nothing of yours to go on. So it fills the gap with the most common way to say it, and that's why the post reads like everyone else's. Editing it back into your voice can take as long as writing it yourself.

The good news

EchoOS starts from you. Setup interviews you to build a voice profile, and you can keep more than one and edit any of them. Drafts start from the transcript of what you said on camera, written in the voice you pick. Every post is a draft you open, revise, approve and copy. You're the last edit, every time.

The bad news

I build around one rule: each piece of content has one job, to start a conversation with the right person. A post that tries to talk to everyone gets a like and a scroll, and nobody has a reason to reply. And when getting one post out takes hours, there's no time left to decide what its job is.

The good news

EchoOS can't pick the job for you, and no tool can promise you clients. What it does is take the slow part off your plate. Your videos become transcripts, clip suggestions and drafts in your voice, so your time goes to what the post says and who it's for.

The bad news

A routine with a handful of manual steps breaks the first busy week. Finding the file, transcribing it, cutting it, writing it up. Every step that needs you is a place it stops, and once it stops, starting again feels like starting from zero.

The good news

EchoOS cuts the steps down. Your videos stay where they already are, and a watched folder brings new ones in on their own, transcribed if you want. Search by topic tag or by what was said. Ask for clips or a post in the same chat, and it keeps the conversation when you switch screens or restart. When you do sit down, you start from a draft.
